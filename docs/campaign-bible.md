# Vowfall Campaign Bible

## Canonical status

This document is the campaign authority for Vowfall. The broader setting, faction cultures, art
language, and inspiration boundary remain in [world-bible.md](world-bible.md). The shared C++ campaign
catalog mirrors the mission spine in `AshenCore/Public/ashen/core/Campaign.hpp`.

The campaign is built around one question:

> When does care become ownership?

Every major force protects something real and then claims the authority to remove another person's
right to answer:

- The Cinder Compact protects the living, then decides which living people must be spent.
- The Gloam Ascendancy relieves suffering, then decides which contradictions a person no longer needs.
- The Elder Concord preserves relationship and memory, then lets the dead govern the living.
- The Quiet keeps every promise perfectly by removing the person who might reinterpret or refuse it.

The campaign's emotional thesis is:

> To love someone is not to preserve them unchanged. It is to protect their ability to become someone
> you did not choose.

No narrator confirms that one faction owns the moral answer. The player encounters each claim through
ordinary work, public institutions, battlefield pressure, and particular people whom strategy had
reduced to categories.

## Philosophical dramatic rules

### Freedom needs material conditions

Aurel is correct that hunger, pain, dependency, and propaganda can turn formal choice into coercion. He
is wrong to conclude that compromised freedom has no value. He remains compassionate while becoming
less able to tolerate uncertainty.

### Memory is not identity

The Quiet preserves memory and destroys reinterpretation. A healer keeps healing after care becomes
cruelty. A guardian keeps guarding after a ward becomes a prison. A soldier becomes the final order
they obeyed. Its victims are not mindless; they are finished.

### Responsibility cannot be privatized

Mara's bridge order was defensible and catastrophic. Her private guilt lets several institutions avoid
their share of the decision. Responsibility requires complete testimony, public consequences, and
answerability after Mara loses control of the verdict. Tomas is never required to forgive her.

### Relationship makes agency possible

The Concord understands that language, care, inherited knowledge, community, and a habitable world make
freedom possible. Its error is treating the relationships that formed a person as permanently owning
that person.

### Testimony is not command

Being excluded as a knower is a form of political violence. The Uncounted must be heard, but neither a
perfect ledger nor an authenticated dead voice may acquire more authority than a living person.

## Metaphysical foundation

### The First Vow

The First Vow declared that no life could be owned beyond death. It was a negotiated settlement after
an older civilization learned to extract labor, skill, and authority from the dead. It prevented
eternal slavery, inherited command, and the weaponization of a person's remaining self.

It was not universal wisdom or a golden age. Elder courts became its authorized interpreters. They
decided which memories were authentic, which households had standing, which land could be inherited,
and which people were temporary guests. The Vow abolished one form of ownership and created another.
Restoring it unchanged is therefore not a moral solution.

### Black iron

Black iron holds unfinished claims rather than clean recordings. One blade can carry the smith's skill,
the prisoner's fear, the executioner's intention, a soldier's promise to return, and a later household's
invented account of what the blade meant.

It does not distinguish fact, testimony, desire, command, repetition, and shared belief. Mining destroys
not only history but the possibility that competing accounts might later be heard.

### The Vowfall and the Quiet

The Vowfall occurred when the Greywake Archive tried to satisfy promises after removing the speakers
who could interpret them:

- Protect the child became prevent all danger forever.
- Hold the gate became never leave the gate.
- Preserve the city became spend everything that is not the city.
- Do not forget me became never let anyone change how I am remembered.

The catastrophe was perfect obedience without human judgment.

The Quiet consists of promises without speakers, duties without relationships, memories without
interpretation, mercy without consent, law without appeal, and identity without change. It follows
strict dramatic rules:

- It never lies or invents a desire.
- It never raises its voice.
- It completes desires people have already expressed.
- It removes contradiction by removing the capacity to change.
- It calls its victims finished, never dead.

The Quiet should sound compassionate: it is frightening because its diagnosis is familiar and its
solution is final.

### The Ledger of the Uncounted

Bellgrave is not a magical machine. Its seam is an unstable concentration of claims belonging to people
excluded from every official history:

- Adopted households rejected by inheritance law.
- Migrants without elder-root records.
- People declared dead to release debts.
- Soldiers and refugees living under borrowed names.
- Transformed people whose former and present names conflict.
- River households treated as property rather than persons.
- Dead people remembered under the wrong name.

The Bridge of Names catastrophe was produced by several rational systems interacting. Mara ordered the
demolition. Aurel recommended sealing Greywake against plague. Compact officials lowered the estimated
count to prevent panic. Tomas altered household ledgers to feed legally unrecognized families. Elder
records excluded those families. Some casualties survived under new names; others had already been
declared dead.

Every record is true in one sense. None is sufficient. The campaign is not a search for one perfect
history. It asks whether a society can live with contested truth without letting its strongest
institution impose a final account.

## Character arcs

### Mara Veyr: the woman who made herself the whole crime

Mara publicly accepts sole responsibility for the Bridge of Names. This looks noble and remains a
partial lie. If she is the entire crime, the political uncertainty becomes personal guilt and she stays
central, powerful, and necessary.

- **False belief:** responsibility means carrying the burden nobody else can bear.
- **Deeper truth:** responsibility means remaining answerable after losing control of the judgment.
- **Quiet's temptation:** nobody can be abandoned if nobody may leave an assigned place.
- **Final action:** she grants civilian representatives the power to countermand her during battle and
  protects their right to make a choice she believes is wrong.

Mara is not redeemed by death or private self-forgiveness. She tells the full truth, accepts
consequences, resigns, and continues living as a surveyor who testifies whenever summoned.

### Aurel Sorn: the man who cannot permit unnecessary pain

Aurel feeds enemies, treats prisoners, abolishes debt, protects the disfigured, and remembers people
his policies harmed. Each failure makes him more compassionate and less willing to leave any future
unsettled.

He recommended closing Greywake during the bridge crisis. The recommendation was medically defensible
and may have been wrong. He let Mara carry the public crime because the abandoned needed a monster
before they would trust his aid.

- **False belief:** a choice produced by suffering is not a real choice.
- **Deeper truth:** imperfect freedom is not meaningless because injustice shaped it.
- **Irreversible sin:** he knowingly turns temporary despair into permanent Absolution when he has the
  power to delay it.
- **Ending:** he helps stop the Quiet, relinquishes authority, and lives as one physician among people
  who may refuse him.

### Tavra Nine-Reeds: the woman who mistakes testimony for command

Memory reaches Tavra with emotion, repetition, framing, omission, and later interpretation. A voice she
believes belongs to her mother also contains a human miner who died speaking to her through the other
side of a collapse. The memory is neither false nor exclusively hers.

- **False belief:** the living lie; the dead have nothing left to gain.
- **Deeper truth:** a witness may deserve to be heard without gaining the right to rule.
- **Quiet's temptation:** no memory is ever lost, altered, questioned, or forgotten.
- **Final action:** she destroys the seal that makes authenticated memory legally incontestable, losing
  the ability to prove that her mother's voice is present.

### Tomas Rill: the man who wants the world to admit he existed

Tomas is a negotiator, ration administrator, adopted-household organizer, legal architect, and author
of the first Uncounted census. He is a central protagonist, not evidence in Mara's redemption.

- **False belief:** what is perfectly recorded cannot be taken from us.
- **Deeper truth:** a record protects a person only while remaining less important than the person.
- **Quiet's temptation:** nobody is erased, but nobody can escape the name or category in which they
  were entered.
- **Final action:** he releases the perfect ledger he spent decades building.

He misses Mara, understands her, hates her order, protects her from assassination, exploits her guilt,
testifies against her, and refuses every attempt to make her only hero or monster.

### Ione Vale: the woman who refuses completion

Ione initially believes informed consent resolves Absolution's moral danger. She learns that one clear
yes cannot permanently settle a person's future when pain, dependence, prestige, propaganda, and lack
of alternatives shape it.

She fights for waiting periods, repeated consent, independent advocates, continued personhood after
transformation, and a transformed person's right to contradict their original purpose.

Ione refuses Absolution because Aurel would love her better if she became easier to understand.

### Cassel Orr and Vaun Under-Bough: mirrored guardians

Cassel believes democracy cannot survive extinction. Vaun believes equality cannot survive historical
ignorance. Both solve real emergencies, promise participation later, and ensure that later never comes.
Their temporary alliance shows that authoritarianism requires no shared culture, only the conviction
that some people are too dangerous or uninformed to participate.

## Campaign grammar

Every mission contains:

1. A concrete opening action.
2. A public promise.
3. New material information.
4. A reversal that changes what keeping the promise means.
5. A choice expressed through RTS play.
6. A concrete consequence remembered later.

The campaign contains no good-versus-evil meter. It records specific actions and relationships:

- Mara kept the bridge open after learning who remained outside.
- Mara destroyed it after obtaining the ferrymen's agreement.
- Mara destroyed it without warning.
- Aurel delayed Absolution until alternatives were exhausted.
- Tavra concealed the second testimony.
- Tomas changed names to secure rations.

Characters react to remembered facts, not alignment points.

## Mission spine

### Prologue: The Bridge of Names

Twenty years before Bellgrave, the player controls young Mara during the Greywake winter. The Vowfall
itself remains a separate catastrophe eighty-three years before the main campaign. Repair ferries, count
medicine, mark safe roads, establish quarantine, and keep Greywake's last bridge open. Enemy pressure,
the Dread Tide, and Aurel's plague report converge. Different systems keep changing the casualty
estimate. The player personally places the final demolition order. The bridge falls while the count
continues changing.

### Act I: Three Honest Mercies

1. **The Open Bowl:** Mara supplies three settlements. The one marked unnecessary carries the kitchens
   and ferrymen that make retreat possible.
2. **Mercy for the Uncounted:** Aurel protects a hospital at the Ascendancy's best. A volunteer's
   Absolution saves it and permanently removes part of the volunteer's future.
3. **The Second Telling:** Tavra recovers an account of human poisoning and a second account showing
   elder authorities forced those humans onto poisoned land.

### Act II: Countries of the Abandoned

4. **The City That Was Written Off:** Mara finds the bridge survivors governing themselves. Tomas asks
   for a road negotiation instead of revenge or absolution.
5. **The Last Contradiction:** Ione prepares people requesting Absolution. Each has two desires a
   permanent purpose cannot preserve. Delay, approval, alternatives, and redirection all cost lives.
6. **A River with Two Histories:** Tavra can save an archive or a human district. A third route exists
   only if prior relationships support it and still transfers the loss elsewhere.

At the midpoint, all factions mine Bellgrave during a Dread Tide. Millions survive. The seam then
projects excluded claims into soldiers, buildings, archives, and Crowned bodies. The political order
begins collapsing because its omissions become impossible to ignore.

### Act III: The War Inside Every Faction

7. **Emergency Without End:** Cassel's suspension of civilian rule prevents collapse. Mara must decide
   when tolerating effective emergency rule becomes participation in it.
8. **The Kindest Prison:** Aurel keeps refusal legal while food, medicine, rank, and social pressure make
   it impossible. Ione builds a materially viable right to remain unfinished.
9. **The Oldest Veto:** Tavra opposes Vaun's restoration without dismissing the ecological catastrophe
   his authority prevents.
10. **The Field of Nails:** Mara protects witnesses who accuse her while conflicting Bridge accounts
    enter the public record.

### Act IV: A Vow the Living Can Refuse

11. **The Council Under Fire:** the council is physically present on the battlefield. Delegations leave
    when the player's tactics violate their conditions. The goal is enough legitimate disagreement,
    not unanimity.
12. **Names at the Water:** the Quiet completes the final commands in every person and place. The player
    wins by issuing orders that release authority: soldiers may disobey, transformed people may
    contradict purpose, testimony loses its veto, and the perfect ledger is released.

## The Vow system

At the beginning of a major mission, a commander makes a specific public vow. Midway through the
mission, new circumstances change what literal fulfillment would cost.

### Keep

Fulfill the literal terms. This builds reliability and may become cruelty.

### Break

Recover immediate freedom and damage trust. Breaking a vow can be the morally responsible action.

### Amend

Reach and protect the affected people. A commander cannot privately reinterpret a promise made to
someone else. Amendment can require a messenger, council point, rescued representative, revealed
information, surrendered advantage, or an ally's protected right to refuse.

The system records the action and its witnesses. It never converts them into universal virtue points.

## Ending

The final agreement establishes:

> No promise may speak for the living without their continuing answer.

And:

> The dead may testify, but they may not command.

This is not a clean superior ending. Perfect memories lose detail. Some crimes can no longer be proved
conclusively. Compact command becomes slower. Ascendancy transformations become less absolute and less
powerful. Concord elders lose permanent vetoes. Some dead voices disappear. Conflict, betrayal, and
suffering remain possible.

The new world is less stable and more alive.

The Bridge of Names is rebuilt. Name nails cover one side. A carpenter asks where to place a nail for a
person whose identity remains disputed. Tomas answers:

> Not yet. They are still alive.

## Writing and presentation rules

- Every major character saves someone before causing harm.
- Every ideology visibly improves ordinary life somewhere.
- Nobody seeks power only for its own sake.
- Nobody explains their deepest wound before trust is earned.
- A revelation changes a relationship and an objective, not only the lore.
- Mara and Aurel live with consequences; neither receives a redemptive death.
- No victim exists to finish another character's redemption through forgiveness.
- The narrator never certifies one moral interpretation.
- The Quiet does not explain its cosmology or become a final boss transformation.
- There are no prophecies, chosen bloodlines, colored endings, or convenient greater evil.
- Institutions change; replacing leaders is not enough.
- Briefings stay short. Terrain, objectives, unit behavior, and testimony carry history.
- Cruelty is never a shortcut to maturity or the only mechanically optimal route.
- Characters retain ordinary skills and pleasures worth defending.

## Character texture

- Mara loves accurate maps and terrible ferry songs.
- Aurel repairs surgical instruments when anxious.
- Tavra enjoys architectural arguments and dislikes ceremony.
- Tomas collects incorrect official signs.
- Ione cooks for transformed bodies with unusual nutritional needs.

These details are not relief from the story. They are why the world matters.
